# Switch model class, not features, to climb

Honest Spaceship Titanic pipelines that reach 0.815+ on the public leaderboard do it by changing the model, not by adding features. Every documented, code-backed result at that level uses **TabPFN v3.5**, a tabular foundation model. It beat an honestly validated CatBoost by about **+0.008 OOF** on the same group-aware folds and scored **0.81786 public on its own**. A nested stack of TabPFN variants scored **0.82020**, and the best of eight CV-ranked candidates scored **0.82604** ([TaeyanG4](https://github.com/TaeyanG4/kaggle-spaceship-titanic-experiments)). GBDT-only pipelines like ours stall at about **0.805-0.811** public no matter how much feature, tuning or ensembling work goes in. Independent projects report the same negative results we found: GBDT blends, pseudo-labels, cabin-neighbour features and threshold shifts all failed. Our 1.4-point CV-to-LB gap is mostly measurement noise. The leaderboard scores all **4,277 test rows** with no private split, so a single reading has a standard error of about **0.6 points**, and our 0.80266 is exactly the board's median. The 0.93-0.965 scores at the top come from no published method and almost certainly reflect leaderboard probing. The legitimate ceiling is about **0.83 OOF and 0.82-0.84 public**. The next step is to run TabPFN v3.5 through our existing StratifiedGroupKFold harness, then blend or stack it. Further GBDT work is not worth the time.

## TabPFN v3.5 explains every honest score above 0.815

The best-documented high score comes from TaeyanG4's October 2026 project, which publishes its full LB history. A CatBoost baseline scored **0.80780**. Moving early stopping to an inner split raised it to **0.81131**. **TabPFN v3.5 alone scored 0.81786**. A nested logistic-regression stack of three TabPFN v3.5 variants (frozen, fine-tuned within folds, fine-tuned then refit) scored **0.82020**, and the best of eight candidates scored **0.82604**, placing 8th with only 12 submissions ([TaeyanG4](https://github.com/TaeyanG4/kaggle-spaceship-titanic-experiments)). Its honest OOF on StratifiedGroupKFold was **0.8291/0.8288/0.8269** across three seeds. TabPFN v3.5 (`tabpfn` 9.1.0, `create_default_for_version(V3_5)`, GPU) averaged **0.8252 OOF against 0.8174 for honest CatBoost** on identical features and folds ([discoveries.md](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/TaeyanG4/kaggle-spaceship-titanic-experiments/main/discoveries.md)). A second, unrelated agent project saw the same effect. Adding TabPFN-3.5 to CatBoost improved CV by +0.0072 to +0.0080 and won **15 of 15 folds**, and public LB rose from **0.80523 to 0.81084**. TabPFN alone (CV 0.82104) slightly beat the blend ([vltnkiz #59](https://github.com/vltnkiz/kaggle-spaceship-titanic/issues/59)). The 0.82604 file was reproduced exactly by a third party who fitted an LR meta-model on TaeyanG4's published member probabilities. That third party's own CatBoost pipeline topped out at **0.80593** ([Ziiimu-H](https://github.com/Ziiimu-H/spaceship-titanic)).

The details of these results matter for planning. The stack's gain came from **variants of the same TabPFN**, not from adding model families. In the stack the frozen model took a negative weight and the fine-tuned model a positive one. Adding CatBoost to the TabPFN blend *cost* 0.0020. XGBoost, LightGBM, ExtraTrees, TabPFN v2.5, TabICL, TabDPT, RealMLP, TabM and TabR all scored below TabPFN v3.5 and were dropped. **The version matters: v2.5 scored about 0.0075 below v3.5**, roughly level with CatBoost ([discoveries.md](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/TaeyanG4/kaggle-spaceship-titanic-experiments/main/discoveries.md)). This fits the wider benchmark picture. Tree models were state of the art on ~10k-row data as recently as 2022 ([Grinsztajn et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2207.08815)), but TabArena now finds that "tabular foundation models dominate for small data" and that **CatBoost gains the least of any model from tuning and ensembling** ([TabArena](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.16791v2)). Our data (8,693 rows, 24 features) fits within TabPFN's ≤10,000-row and ≤500-feature limits.

The GBDT write-ups closest to ours cluster tightly. malcolmlow's 5-fold CatBoost with deterministic imputation, `Cabin_Region = Cabin_Num // 300` and grouped spend scored **OOF 0.8163, public 0.80967**. Adding a 10-fold HistGB blend at threshold 0.48 raised OOF to 0.8205 but **dropped LB to 0.80547** ([DEV](https://dev.to/malcolmlow/pocket-data-science-ii-reaching-the-kaggle-spaceship-titanic-top-6-on-android-with-antigravity-p4a)). Other GBDT and NN results include an LGBM+CatBoost vote at 0.80897 ([xup65k6t6](https://xup65k6t6.github.io/Personal_Blog/projects/Kaggle:Spaceship-Titanic)), a three-layer MLP at 0.80851 ([acciochris](https://acciochris.github.io/posts/spaceship-nn.html)) and CatBoost at 0.80687 ([Danial-Rashidi](https://github.com/Danial-Rashidi/spaceship-titanic-transport-prediction)), all below 0.81. Only two feature ideas from the strong notebooks are missing from our tried list: **SurnameSize counted over train+test** and **unsupervised KNN-distance features**, both in TaeyanG4's champion feature set, which publishes no construction details. Our `GroupSize` is already computed on the combined frame, but our base features have no family-size count. Treat these as marginal. TaeyanG4 also found that *supervised* kNN features cost **0.0086** ([discoveries.md](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/TaeyanG4/kaggle-spaceship-titanic-experiments/main/discoveries.md)), which matches our failed cabin-neighbour rate.

One caveat applies to the whole TabPFN case. The strongest numbers come from **a single, self-reported source** whose findings are marked as pending review. TabPFN v3.5's licensing, API access and GPU requirements were not researched, and they need checking before we commit.

## Our 1.4-point gap is mostly noise, and the usual culprits do not apply

The leaderboard has no public/private split. Every one of the **326 distinct scores** on the November 2026 board equals k/4,277 truncated to five decimals, and no other denominator fits. Our score is exactly **3,433 of 4,277 rows**. The competition rules state that final submission selection "is not relevant" ([Rules](https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/spaceship-titanic/rules)), and a practitioner independently states that scoring uses the whole test set ([TaeyanG4](https://github.com/TaeyanG4/kaggle-spaceship-titanic-experiments)). One research note assumed a 50% split, but the score-granularity evidence settles the question. The binomial standard error of an LB reading near 0.80 is therefore **0.61 points**, or about 0.64 after accounting for group clustering. The standard error of our OOF is about 0.41-0.54 points. Our gap of 1.42 points sits at **z ≈ 1.8-1.9**: borderline, not proof of a bug ([vltnkiz #59](https://github.com/vltnkiz/kaggle-spaceship-titanic/issues/59) independently gives ±0.006). Context helps here. **0.80266 is exactly the median team score**, shared by 22 teams, and 1,104 teams sit in the 0.800-0.810 band. Run-to-run LB spread is wide too: one Kaggle thread reports eight runs of the same agent setup scoring **0.80230-0.82069** ([discussion 712563](https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/spaceship-titanic/discussion/712563)).

The usual explanations do not fit our setup. The best-documented leak in this competition is **early stopping on the scored fold**, which inflated CV by about 0.004 and cost LB 0.0035 ([TaeyanG4](https://github.com/TaeyanG4/kaggle-spaceship-titanic-experiments)). Our `catboost_fit_predict` uses a fixed 1,000 iterations with no eval set, so that leak does not apply. **Selection optimism**, meaning picking the best of many configurations on the same folds, is the next most cited cause ([Basiru-Haruna](https://github.com/Basiru-Haruna/spaceship-titanic-ml)). It does not apply either, because we submitted our first configuration. **Group leakage in the fold split** is tiny here, with group-blind and group-aware CV differing by 0.0008 ([vltnkiz #59](https://github.com/vltnkiz/kaggle-spaceship-titanic/issues/59)), and we already split by group. **Threshold and class balance** cannot account for it either. We predict 51.4% True against a train rate of 50.4%, a gap of only about 43 rows, and the one careful study of threshold tuning found +0.0009 on CatBoost and −0.0023 on TabPFN ([discoveries.md](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/TaeyanG4/kaggle-spaceship-titanic-experiments/main/discoveries.md)). Our imputation runs on train+test combined before CV, but it uses no labels and test rows receive identical treatment, so it is not a CV-to-test leak. One structural mismatch does remain. **Our submission is a full-train refit** (`fit_full_predict`), while the OOF number measures fold models. A refit with fixed iterations on 25% more data should be at least as good, but the 25-model fold average is the thing our CV actually measured.

Honest pipelines elsewhere show smaller gaps: about −0.005 for honest CatBoost ([TaeyanG4](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/TaeyanG4/kaggle-spaceship-titanic-experiments/main/discoveries.md)), −0.003 for MAMUT's CatBoost ([MAMUT](https://mamut.readthedocs.io/en/latest/_sources/kaggle_benchmarks.rst.txt)) and −0.005 for a group-aware CatBoost ([Basiru-Haruna](https://github.com/Basiru-Haruna/spaceship-titanic-ml)). So roughly 0.5 points of our gap is the normal, systematic CV-to-LB drop for this competition, and the rest fits an unlucky draw. That still does not justify resubmitting the same model. The 4,277 labels are fixed, so the same predictions always return the same score. Two of our models differ on LB only through the rows where they disagree. **The only way to move the number is to change predictions with a model that is genuinely better on CV.**

## The 0.93+ scores come from probing, not modelling

The live board shows **0.96539 and 0.93149 at ranks 1-2**, then a cliff to **0.83867** at rank 3, with ranks 3-20 packed between 0.828 and 0.839 ([Leaderboard](https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/spaceship-titanic/leaderboard)). The dataset is fictional and Kaggle has never released the test labels or the generating process. No labelled test file exists in Kaggle Datasets or on GitHub ([Welcome thread](https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/spaceship-titanic/discussion/309323)). That leaves leaderboard probing or copied answer files as the plausible routes to 0.93+. Probing is easy in principle here. Because the full test set is scored to five decimals, flipping one prediction moves the score by a visible 0.00023, and 10 submissions a day is enough to correct the roughly 580 rows that separate 0.83 from 0.965 within weeks. Users raised exactly this in the staff thread in 2023 ([#309319](https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/spaceship-titanic/discussion/309319)). Community replies to a 0.96 first place in 2024 ("probably some leak or cheating") and a 1.000 score ("fishy") drew no staff response ([#499696](https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/spaceship-titanic/discussion/499696), [#530046](https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/spaceship-titanic/discussion/530046)). TaeyanG4 audited high-scoring public notebooks and excluded ones that embed "external answer files" or a hardcoded "best public override". It also found that a popular notebook showing **0.81833** has an honest OOF of only about **0.805** ([TaeyanG4](https://github.com/TaeyanG4/kaggle-spaceship-titanic-experiments)). No specific team has been shown to probe, so this conclusion is inference, but it is strong inference.

Several independent signals point to a legitimate ceiling of about **0.83 expected accuracy**. The best carefully validated OOF anywhere is about 0.831. Rows that the model gets wrong across seeds are "confidently wrong", and removing label noise did not help ([TaeyanG4](https://github.com/TaeyanG4/kaggle-spaceship-titanic-experiments)). A 2026 forum post states plainly that "the public LB tops out around 0.83" ([#560289](https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/spaceship-titanic/discussion/560289)). Ranks 3-20 at 0.83-0.84 are a mix of strong models and lucky picks. The teams just above TaeyanG4 had made 81-82 submissions, and taking the best of many readings with an SD of 0.006 inflates the maximum. On the November board, **0.810 is roughly rank 220 and 0.815 roughly rank 120**, so a TabPFN-level 0.818 would place us around the top 5%.

## Neural nets, AutoML and threshold tricks add nothing over CatBoost

Apart from TabPFN v3.5, the alternatives match the CatBoost plateau or fall below it. Plain MLPs score 0.79-0.81 and TabNet about 0.80 (the TabNet figure comes from a search snippet only) ([acciochris](https://acciochris.github.io/posts/spaceship-nn.html), [defcodeking](https://www.kaggle.com/code/defcodeking/tabnet-a-neural-network-for-tabular-data/notebook)). Modern deep tabular models that rank highly on TabArena (RealMLP, TabM, TabR) scored **0.02-0.04 below TabPFN v3.5** here ([discoveries.md](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/TaeyanG4/kaggle-spaceship-titanic-experiments/main/discoveries.md)). AutoGluon reached 0.809-0.811 public after an 11-hour run, according to a snippet only, and H2O AutoML reached "> 0.79" ([cozyhn](https://www.kaggle.com/code/cozyhn/autogluon-spaceship-titanic), [vytautas-bunevicius](https://github.com/vytautas-bunevicius/kaggle-spaceship-titanic)). Stacks of diverse weak learners only look good when the baseline is weak. One 10-model stack gained 1.3 points, but over a 0.800 random forest ([polarr/titan](https://github.com/polarr/titan)). Oracle analysis over five GBDT-style models put combiner headroom at about zero ([vltnkiz #59](https://github.com/vltnkiz/kaggle-spaceship-titanic/issues/59)), which matches our 0.98-0.99 prediction correlations. In post-processing, group-consistency rules cost about 0.002, pseudo-labelling cost 0.0005 and CatBoost seed bagging added only 0.0006 ([discoveries.md](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/TaeyanG4/kaggle-spaceship-titanic-experiments/main/discoveries.md)). The one stacking pattern that worked was a **nested LR meta-model over logits of comparably strong but different members**, trained per fold only on the other folds' OOF predictions. It added +0.0031 OOF, confirmed on fresh seeds. No study of Platt or isotonic calibration for this competition exists. With a near-50% base rate, calibration would leave the decision boundary at 0.5 anyway.

## Prioritized action plan for this repo

The plan runs in order of expected value per hour. It keeps the existing harness (`multi_seed_cv`, `cv_splits`, 5 eval seeds) as the only arbiter. Promotion requires a gain of at least **+0.003 OOF that is positive on at least 4 of 5 seeds**. Fold std is about 0.012, so anything smaller is not real.

| # | Action | Where | Expected effect | Stop rule |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Add `tabpfn_fit_predict` (tabpfn ≥9.1, `create_default_for_version(V3_5)`) to `app/modeling.py` on the same 24 features. Pass the raw categoricals. Confirm licence, API and GPU needs first. | `app/modeling.py`, `model.ipynb` | +0.007-0.008 OOF; LB ~0.81-0.818 | Drop if < +0.003 OOF, or if only v2.x is available (v2.5 ≈ CatBoost) |
| 2 | Blend TabPFN + CatBoost OOF (reuse `ensemble_result`), then fit a nested LR stack on logits where each fold's meta-model sees only other folds' OOF. Add a fine-tuned TabPFN member if fine-tuning is available. | `app/modeling.py` | +0.002-0.003 OOF over TabPFN alone; LB ~0.82 | Keep TabPFN alone if CatBoost gets ≤0 weight (as in TaeyanG4) |
| 3 | Submit the 25-model fold-averaged test probabilities alongside the full refit, and log how many rows the two disagree on. | `fit_full_predict`, `write_submission` | Aligns the submission with what OOF measured; bounded by the disagreement count | One submission; never select between them on LB alone |
| 4 | Cheap checks: adversarial validation on the *engineered* features (target AUC ≈ 0.5), and an OOF accuracy curve over thresholds 0.45-0.55 | notebook | Confirms no hidden shift; expected flat, so keep 0.5 | Act only if AUC > 0.55 or the threshold gain is > 0.2 pt |
| 5 | Marginal features, tested on TabPFN, not CatBoost: `SurnameSize` over train+test, `Cabin_num // 300` region, unsupervised KNN-distance features | `app/preprocessing.py` | 0 to +0.002 | Drop on any non-positive multi-seed result |
| 6 | Stop all further GBDT tuning, GBDT ensembling, pseudo-labelling, label-based neighbour or group features, threshold shifting and group post-processing | — | Saves time; each is a documented null or negative result | — |

Submission discipline matters as much as modelling. Choose the candidate on multi-seed OOF, submit once or twice, and treat any LB difference under about **0.01** as uninformative. TaeyanG4's LB order did not follow its CV order, and bagged copies of one model differed by 0.0028 ([TaeyanG4](https://github.com/TaeyanG4/kaggle-spaceship-titanic-experiments)). A realistic target is **0.815-0.825 public**. Anything above about 0.835 would take luck or practices we should not adopt.

## Conclusion

The useful reframing is that our pipeline is not underperforming. It sits on the GBDT ceiling, and our leaderboard reading drew the unlucky side of a ±0.6-point measurement. The ten experiments that failed in rounds 2-3 were not bad ideas. They were correctly measuring a plateau that several independent teams also hit and documented. The remaining legitimate headroom of about 1-2 points comes from a model with different inductive biases, and in 2026 that means a recent tabular foundation model, not more trees.

The main open risk is evidential. Nearly all of the TabPFN v3.5 upside traces to one self-reported project, with only partial confirmation from a second, and its practical requirements are unverified. Our own harness can settle the question at low cost: one new `fit_predict` function evaluated on folds we already trust. If TabPFN v3.5 does not beat CatBoost by about 0.003 OOF there, the honest conclusion is that this repo is finished at roughly 0.81 expected accuracy, and further leaderboard climbing would be noise-chasing.
